# Product

## Product thesis

MzansiCommute creates a practical operating record for minibus taxi businesses.

The immediate user value is visibility and accountability.

The longer-term commercial value is that verified, consented operational data may support products and decisions that are currently difficult to price or justify.

## Adoption sequence

### 1. Taxi owners

Primary early adopter.

Core needs:
- fare verification;
- driver accountability;
- route visibility;
- maintenance evidence;
- financial history that can eventually be read by an insurer or lender.

### 2. Taxi associations

Scale and governance layer.

Potential value:
- corridor activity evidence;
- route-management support;
- dispute-resolution support;
- stronger negotiating evidence with municipalities and partners.

### 3. Commuters

Transaction participant, not initial buyer.

Potential value:
- optional cashless fare flow;
- recorded trip event;
- greater payment convenience.

## Commercial thesis

### Pilot

No direct customer charge is assumed in the working model.

Owners receive visibility and records while MzansiCommute validates whether the data can become useful.

### Potential later revenue

- Insurers: verified, consented fleet-risk insights.
- Associations: route/governance tools.
- Financial services: products supported by verified income history.
- Public sector: appropriately aggregated/anonymised corridor insights.

These are hypotheses and future commercial paths, not current revenue claims.

## Product boundaries

Do not assume that MzansiCommute is:
- a commuter route-planning app;
- a ride-hailing marketplace;
- a replacement for association governance;
- an insurance company;
- a bank;
- a surveillance or enforcement tool.

## Current website purpose

The current public site is a pre-pilot trust surface for:
- taxi owners and associations;
- insurers and financial partners;
- research/public-sector partners.

It should establish credibility without pretending the pilot has already happened.
