# Copy Guide

## Voice

MzansiCommute speaks like someone who understands both operational reality and institutional language.

The voice is:
- direct;
- factual;
- confident;
- low-hype;
- warm without being cute;
- specific where evidence exists.

## Language

Use South African English:
- organise;
- colour;
- centre;
- programme where appropriate.

## Claims

Every quantitative statement must be one of:
1. a verified internal result;
2. a sourced published statistic;
3. an explicitly labelled target.

Never blur these categories.

## Product tense

Use present tense only for capabilities that exist now.

Use future or conditional language for:
- post-pilot features;
- insurer products;
- financial-service products;
- public-sector data products;
- scale that has not happened.

## Avoid

- revolutionary;
- world-class;
- industry-leading;
- game-changer;
- "disrupting the taxi industry";
- "formalising the informal sector";
- unsupported safety claims;
- unsupported insurer/lender claims.

## Preferred framing

Instead of:
> We transform the taxi industry with cutting-edge data.

Prefer:
> We build a usable operating record from the activity already happening every day.

Instead of:
> Unlock better insurance.

Prefer:
> Verified operating records could give insurers better evidence to evaluate risk.

## Founder story

The founder working document contains a vivid origin story with named individuals and a specific rank/location.

Treat that story as **internal until founder review confirms what can be published safely**.

Do not automatically move names, exact places, or identifying circumstances into public copy.
