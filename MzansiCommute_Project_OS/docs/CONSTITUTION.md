# MzansiCommute Constitution

This document defines non-negotiable principles. Product decisions, code changes, partnerships, design choices, and AI-agent behaviour should be judged against them.

## Article I — The industry is not the problem

MzansiCommute starts from the premise that the taxi industry is not defective because it is informal.

The problem is the absence of infrastructure that makes existing activity legible, verifiable, and useful to the people who already create the value.

We do not design from contempt for informality.

## Article II — Build with, never at

The company must not behave like an outsider arriving to "fix" an industry it has not earned the trust to change.

Association structures, owner realities, driver realities, rank operations, and local context are constraints to understand, not obstacles to route around.

## Article III — Utility before extraction

Data must emerge from a product that gives users direct value.

We do not build data collection systems whose only meaningful beneficiary is an insurer, lender, investor, government body, or MzansiCommute itself.

If operators do not receive a clear benefit, the data proposition is incomplete.

## Article IV — Consent is architecture

Consent is not a paragraph in a privacy policy.

It must shape:
- what data is collected;
- when it is collected;
- how it is explained;
- how long it is retained;
- who may access it;
- how it is aggregated or anonymised;
- how permissions can change.

No commercial partnership overrides this principle.

## Article V — Claims must be true at the moment they are read

Targets are targets.  
Results are results.  
Published statistics require a source.

The product must never imply traction, partnerships, deployment scale, security guarantees, or product capabilities that do not yet exist.

## Article VI — Founder safety outranks marketing

The public product must not expose founder-identifying information where doing so creates avoidable physical or operational risk.

No name, portrait, personal biography, school, neighbourhood, association membership, private contact details, or identifying visual motif should be introduced merely to make the startup feel more personal.

## Article VII — Performance is inclusion

A beautiful product that works only on flagship devices and strong connections is not accessible enough for this market.

Core flows must remain usable on constrained networks and modest devices.

Animation is enhancement, never infrastructure.

## Article VIII — Accessibility is part of product quality

Keyboard access, focus visibility, semantic structure, readable contrast, form labelling, and reduced-motion support are not optional polish.

## Article IX — Security is a product property

Secrets do not belong in the repository.  
Personal data is not debugging material.  
Logs must not casually expose user-submitted data.  
Infrastructure changes require deliberate review.

## Article X — The commuter is not the payer by default

The working commercial thesis is that commuter activity creates useful operational data while monetisation comes later from organisations that can pay for higher-order value.

This thesis may evolve, but the product must not quietly shift costs onto commuters without an explicit decision.

## Article XI — We earn the right to scale

Association trust, route-level validation, accurate data, and real operator value come before national ambition.

Scale without legitimacy is fragility.

## Article XII — AI agents are contributors, not authorities

AI coding agents may propose, implement, test, and document.

They must not silently:
- change product strategy;
- change privacy promises;
- relax security controls;
- expose founder identity;
- relabel targets as achievements;
- alter deployment infrastructure;
- introduce new external data-sharing pathways.

Human product ownership remains authoritative.
