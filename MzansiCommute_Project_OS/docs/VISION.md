# Vision

## Problem

South Africa's minibus taxi industry is economically significant and essential to daily mobility, yet large parts of its operation remain undocumented.

The working founder document describes:
- cash fares with little or no durable transaction record;
- limited trip logging;
- limited vehicle-health visibility;
- owners lacking verified operating records;
- associations relying heavily on memory and relationships;
- insurers, lenders, and planners lacking dependable operational evidence.

The underlying thesis is:

> The problem is not the industry. The problem is that the industry has never had a way to make itself legible on its own terms.

## Future state

MzansiCommute aims toward a South African transport economy where taxi operators can bring verified operational evidence into conversations with insurers, lenders, associations, and public-sector bodies.

Examples of the desired future include:
- an owner presenting route, fare, and maintenance records when negotiating insurance;
- an association using corridor data when engaging a municipality;
- risk and financial products priced from real evidence rather than assumption;
- public planning informed by actual movement patterns.

## Long-form vision

Build the trusted data infrastructure layer that allows South Africa's taxi industry to show the economic activity it already creates, without losing control of the terms on which that data is used.

## Working one-line vision

> Every operator seen. Every trip counted. Every rand protected.

This line should be treated as a brand direction, not as a literal promise that every operator, trip, or rand is currently covered by the product.
